## Step 1 => Gathering the Text (Getting the Data)

1. Use pre-built public datasets.
2. Web scraping.
3. APIs.
4. Manual collection / Crowdsourcing.
5. Use NLP tools to automatically generate data (Text Augmentation).

## Step 2 => Text Cleaning

1. Convert all text to lowercase.
2. Remove punctuation in ML tasks (e.g., `@#$%^&*`, etc.).
3. Remove numbers (optional, depending on the task).
4. Remove URLs/Links.
5. Remove HTML tags.
6. Remove emojis and special characters.
7. Remove stopwords (e.g., `is`, `the`, `was`, `and`, `in`).
8. Spelling correction (optional).

**Example:**

Original:
`"I LOVE this movie!!! Visit https://example.com 😊"`

After cleaning:
`"i love this movie"`

## Step 3 => Feature Extraction / Vectorization

Converting text into numerical form so that machine learning models can understand it.

### 1. One-Hot Encoding

Each word is represented as a vector containing `0` and `1`.

**Example:**

Vocabulary:
`["I", "love", "Python"]`

`I` → `[1, 0, 0]`
`love` → `[0, 1, 0]`
`Python` → `[0, 0, 1]`

**Pros:**

* Intuitive and easy to understand.
* Easy to implement.

**Cons:**

* **Sparsity:** Creates large matrices with many zeros.
* **OOV (Out of Vocabulary):** Cannot handle unseen words.
* **Size:** Vocabulary size can become very large.
* **No semantic meaning:** Doesn't understand relationships between words.

---

### 2. Bag of Words (BoW)

Represents text based on the frequency of words in a document. It ignores the order of words.

**Example:**

Sentence:
`"I love Python and I love AI"`

Vocabulary:
`["I", "love", "Python", "and", "AI"]`

BoW:
`[2, 2, 1, 1, 1]`

**Pros:**

* Easy to implement.
* Fixed-size vectors.
* Works well with traditional Machine Learning models.
* Fast and efficient.

**Cons:**

* Sparse matrix.
* Out of Vocabulary problem.
* Doesn't capture semantic meaning well.
* Ignores word order.

### N-Gram

An N-Gram is a sequence of `N` consecutive words. It can also be called a **Bag of N-Grams** when used as features.

**Example:**

Sentence:
`"I love machine learning"`

**Unigram (1-Gram):**
`I`, `love`, `machine`, `learning`

**Bigram (2-Gram):**
`I love`, `love machine`, `machine learning`

**Pros:**

* Captures some word order and context.
* Provides slightly better semantic information than simple BoW.

**Cons:**

* Dimensions increase quickly.
* Out of Vocabulary problem.

---

### 3. TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency)

TF-IDF gives higher importance to words that are frequent in a particular document but less frequent across the entire collection of documents.

**TF (Term Frequency):**

`TF = Number of occurrences of a term in a document / Total number of terms in the document`

**Example:**

Document:
`"I love Python. Python is easy."`

The word `Python` appears **2 times** out of **6 words**.

`TF(Python) = 2/6`

**IDF (Inverse Document Frequency):**

`IDF = log(Total number of documents / Number of documents containing the term)`

A common word appearing in many documents gets a lower IDF, while a rare word gets a higher IDF.

**Pros:**

* Useful for information retrieval.
* Gives more importance to meaningful/rare words.

**Cons:**

* Sparse matrix.
* Out of Vocabulary problem.
* High dimensionality.
* Does not fully capture semantic meaning.
